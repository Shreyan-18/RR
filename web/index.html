Methodology & Mathematical Engine

The Core Analytics Philosophy

Context-First Evaluation

Traditional cricket statistics suffer from severe context-blindness: smashing a boundary when needing 15 runs off the final 4 deliveries of an IPL final carries infinitely greater consequence than hitting one in the powerplay of a dead rubber.

Our quantitative framework reconstructs the true leverage of all 200,999 deliveries by isolating two distinct forces: high-stakes macro match consequence and live micro in-game leverage. By benchmarking each player's execution in high-pressure crunch time against their routine baseline performance, we objectively separate world-class clutch performers from flat-track accumulators.

1

The Composite Pressure Index

Every legal delivery is assigned an instantaneous pressure value from 0.00 to 1.00 uniting two independent forces:

40% Macro Match Stakes + 60% Live Match Leverage
  • Macro Stakes (40%): Pre-game tournament context, knockout urgency, opponent quality, and venue hostility.
  • Live In-Game Leverage (60%): Ball-by-ball tension driven by dynamic required run rate, balls remaining, and wickets lost.
  • Super Overs: Automatically scored at maximum intensity (1.00 Pressure).
  • The 70th Percentile Cutoff: Deliveries with a pressure index of 0.70 or higher isolate the High-Pressure Regime (60,314 deliveries).
2

Macro Stakes Taxonomy

Calculated before the delivery is bowled to reflect the severity of match consequences:

50% Match Consequence + 35% Opposition Tier + 15% Away Fortress
  • Championship Finals (1.00 Weight): 15 winner-take-all tournament finals.
  • Direct Knockouts (0.95 Weight): 9 sudden-death eliminators and semi-finals.
  • Tied Series Deciders (0.85 Weight): 34 winner-take-all bilateral deciders tied at 1–1 or 2–2.
  • Rivalries (+0.15 Boost): IND VS PAK, MI vs CSK, RCB VS CSK, AUS VS ENG.
  • Dead Rubbers (0.35 Weight): 64 low-leverage matches played after series or playoff qualification was secured.

Psychological Momentum Indicators

  • Post-Boundary Stress (Composure): Evaluates what a bowler executes on the ball immediately following a 4 or 6. Jasprit Bumrah (46.8%) and Sunil Narine (40.0%) lead the tournament in comeback dot and wicket rate.
  • Post-Wicket Leverage Surge: Incoming batters face an immediate +8% pressure surge for the next 6 balls as fielding momentum rises.
  • 3-Match Rolling Form: Incorporates a 3-match recent trajectory to account for match fitness, confidence, and fatigue.
5

Performance Scoring Equations & Weights

Batting Performance Equation

Score = 30% Strike Rate + 25% Boundary% + 18% Strike Rotation + 17% Average + 10% Dot Avoidance

Prioritizes Boundary Conversion (25%) over strike rotation (18%) per franchise rules, since high-pressure death overs demand maximum boundaries.

Bowling Performance Equation

Score = 30% Economy + 25% Wicket Strike Rate + 20% Post-Boundary Composure + 15% Dot% + 10% Opposition Tier

Rewards low economy (30%) and wicket-taking strike rate (25%) alongside post-boundary bounce-back composure (20%).

Pressure Performance Ratio (PPR)

Measures how much a player elevates compared to normal conditions: PPR = High-Pressure Score / Baseline Score. A ratio of 1.51x (achieved by Naman Dhir) represents a +51% performance surge under pressure, while a ratio of 0.79x (e.g. Sam Curran as a bowler) represents a -21% decline under pressure.
